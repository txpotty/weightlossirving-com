    Understanding Low Desire in Arlington

    Low sexual desire is one of the most common sexual health concerns women report. When it is persistent, causes distress and is not explained by another condition or medication, it may be diagnosed as hypoactive sexual desire disorder, or HSDD. Causes usually overlap. Antidepressants, hormonal birth control, perimenopause, thyroid problems, pain, poor sleep and relationship strain can all contribute. A good evaluation looks at each.

    Low Libido Treatment for Women

    Treatment starts with finding what changed. Your provider reviews medications known to lower desire, asks about menopause symptoms, thyroid history and pain with sex, and looks at sleep and stress. Simple fixes come first. Treating vaginal dryness or adjusting an SSRI with your prescriber can make a real difference before any new medication is added. Medication options follow if needed.

    Flibanserin for Women in Arlington

    Flibanserin, sold as Addyi, is FDA-approved for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD. The dose is one 100 mg tablet each night at bedtime, and it works on serotonin and dopamine rather than hormones. Results build slowly. Providers usually reassess at eight weeks and stop it if there is no benefit. Alcohol timing matters with flibanserin.

    PT-141 for Women

    PT-141, or bremelanotide, is FDA-approved as Vyleesi for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD. You inject 1.75 mg under the skin of the abdomen or thigh at least 45 minutes ahead of sex, and it works on brain receptors tied to desire. Nausea is common at first. Use it no more than once in 24 hours or eight times a month. Compounded versions are not FDA-approved.

    Oxytocin Therapy in Arlington

    Oxytocin is a hormone involved in bonding, touch and arousal. Compounded oxytocin is sometimes prescribed as a nasal spray or troche taken before intimacy to support arousal and closeness. This use is off-label. There is no FDA approval for this use, the evidence is thin and responses differ a lot from woman to woman. Your provider explains the evidence honestly.

    How a Provider Chooses Between Options

    Menopausal status comes first. Both FDA-approved drugs are labeled for premenopausal women, which means postmenopausal women often get a different plan. Then comes routine. Regular drinkers often lean toward PT-141, women with blood pressure issues toward flibanserin, and liver function shapes the choice too. Your preferences count as well.

    Side Effects and Safety in Arlington

    Flibanserin can cause dizziness, sleepiness, nausea and low blood pressure. Alcohol and CYP3A4-blocking drugs make those effects worse, which explains the bedtime dose and drinking rules. Nausea is common with PT-141. Flushing, headache, a short blood pressure rise and skin darkening with frequent use are also possible. Oxytocin can cause headache or nasal irritation.
