    How Naltrexone Gum Works for Patients in Dallas, TX

    How Naltrexone Gum Works in Dallas

    Naltrexone blocks opioid receptors, and the 50 mg tablet has been FDA-approved for alcohol and opioid use disorder for decades. In gum form, a compounding pharmacy blends a measured dose into a chewable base. Some of the drug may be absorbed through the lining of the mouth while you chew, and the rest is swallowed with saliva. That is the idea. Nobody has shown the gum works better than a tablet.

    Who Naltrexone Gum Is For

    Adults who would be reasonable candidates for naltrexone but dislike swallowing pills are the main group. A few patients want it in a low dose wellness role. Others want help with cravings as part of a broader plan, and like the idea of chewing a piece before a situation where cravings usually hit, such as a dinner out or the drive home from work. Opioid users are not candidates. Significant liver disease usually points to a different plan.

    Dosing and Use in Dallas

    Your provider sets the strength of each piece and how often to use it. Most plans have you chew slowly for a set time, often around 20 to 30 minutes, then throw the piece away. Some plans use one piece daily at the same time. Targeted plans use a piece shortly before a known trigger, with a written cap on how many pieces a day. Compounded strengths vary, so follow the label exactly.

    What to Expect in the First Weeks

    The first week is mostly about learning the routine. Mild nausea and headache are common early on with naltrexone in any format, and a handful of people sleep lightly for a few nights. Most of it settles by week two. The taste takes some getting used to. At a check-in around week two, your provider asks how many pieces you are using, whether the timing works and whether your gums feel sore.

    Side Effects of Naltrexone Gum in Dallas

    The side effects match other forms of naltrexone and include nausea, headache, dizziness and trouble sleeping. Chewing brings a couple of extras, such as a bitter aftertaste and sore gums. Opioids are the serious concern. Naltrexone can stop opioid pain medicine from working and can trigger withdrawal in anyone who uses opioids, which is why the screening is so specific. Yellow eyes, dark urine or bad upper belly pain need a same-day message.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your questionnaire asks about current medications, opioid use, liver health, pregnancy and breastfeeding, plus any major medical conditions. Tramadol and codeine cough syrup are asked about by name. If your history suggests a liver question, the provider may ask you to upload a recent liver panel with ALT and AST before writing anything. Jaw trouble matters too. People with jaw pain or recent dental work are often pointed to capsules instead.

    How Naltrexone Gum Compares in Dallas

    Standard naltrexone tablets have decades of research and FDA approval for alcohol and opioid use disorder. Low dose naltrexone capsules are compounded and used off-label at 1.5 to 4.5 mg. The gum is a newer compounded format with limited published data, chosen mostly for convenience or because swallowing pills is a problem. Weight loss is a different story. For medical weight loss, GLP-1 drugs such as semaglutide have far stronger trial evidence than any naltrexone format.
